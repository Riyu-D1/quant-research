# Phase 11 literature review: what has credible out-of-sample evidence?

Scope: five families the phase-11 brief named, read for one question.
Does the idea survive outside the sample that produced it, after costs, in
large US stocks? Grades follow `docs/PHASE9_LIT_REVIEW.md`:

- **A**: credible post-publication or out-of-sample evidence over long
  samples or many markets, with realistic costs.
- **B**: mixed evidence, or evidence that does not transfer cleanly to a
  long-only, 50%-SPY, S&P 500 book.
- **C**: in-sample, short-window or single-market evidence only.

Phases 9 and 10 already tested and rejected, on this repo's data:
sleeve breadth, sleeve risk parity, vol-managed and trend-gated sleeves,
monthly IC-weighted factor stacks, purged LightGBM, ensembles, FinBERT
headline sentiment, multi-asset trend, dual momentum, risk parity, sector
rotation, low residual vol, short-term reversal, PEAD and a crash overlay.
None of them is re-run. The ideas below are either new to the repo or a
different construction of an old one, and the difference is stated.

## 1. Value/quality composites (QVAL-style)

| reference | finding | grade |
|---|---|---|
| Novy-Marx (2013, JFE), "The other side of value" | Gross profit / assets predicts returns about as well as B/M, and it hedges value. Holds in large caps. | A |
| Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen (2019, RAS), "Quality minus junk" | A profitability + growth + safety composite earns a premium in 24 countries; quality does best in drawdowns. | A |
| Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013, JF), "Value and momentum everywhere" | Value and momentum are negatively correlated in every asset class, so a combination beats either one. | A |
| Gray & Carlisle (2012), *Quantitative Value*; QVAL methodology | EBIT/TEV is the best single value measure in their tests; screen the cheapest decile, then rank by quality. Practitioner work; QVAL itself lagged the market for most of 2015-2021. | B |
| Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020, RFS), "Replicating anomalies"; Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023, JF), "Is there a replication crisis in finance?" | Most accounting-based anomalies shrink once microcaps are value-weighted, but profitability and value replicate in both. | A |
| McLean & Pontiff (2016, JF) | Anomaly returns fall about a third after publication. | A |
| Israel, Laursen & Richardson (2021, JPM), "Is (systematic) value investing dead?" | Value's 2018-2020 drawdown came from spreads widening, not from a broken signal. | B |

**Use here.** The repo's vq leg (B/M, E/P, ROE) is already a value/quality
signal. The new piece is an EBIT/TEV value score plus gross profitability
and ROA, built point-in-time from SEC XBRL `filed` dates. It is tested two
ways. One is a 20-name QVAL-like sleeve. The other is a one-third tilt
inside the B50 ranking. Expect a long-run premium of a few percent a year
at best, with value's long dry spells.

**Holdout caveat.** VLUE (+42%) and QVAL (+29.5%) led the phase-10
holdout, which is the same window phase 11 seals. That result is in the
brief, so the idea of a value tilt is informed by the holdout. A value
tilt that wins on this holdout is therefore weak evidence. The DSR over
the full sample, which includes 2017-2025 when value mostly lagged, is
the check that matters.

## 2. LLM-scored text (filings, calls, news), scored point-in-time

| reference | finding | grade |
|---|---|---|
| Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen (2020, JF), "Lazy prices" | Firms whose 10-K/10-Q text changes a lot from the prior year underperform for months; the effect is strongest in risk-factor sections, with little decay over a 1995-2014 sample. No LLM needed: cosine similarity on word counts. | A/B |
| Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023), "Can ChatGPT forecast stock price movements?" | GPT headline scores predict next-day returns, mostly in small caps, and the edge shrinks as LLM adoption rises. Wrong horizon for a monthly book; phase 9's FinBERT result agrees. | B |
| Chen, Kelly & Xiu (2022), "Expected returns and large language models" | Embeddings of news text predict returns across 16 markets; larger models do better; one-day to one-week horizon. | B |
| Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev (2024), "Financial statement analysis with large language models" | GPT-4 predicts the direction of earnings from anonymised statements better than analysts. The test years sit inside the model's training data. | C |
| Glasserman & Lin (2023), "Assessing look-ahead bias in stock return predictions generated by GPT sentiment analysis"; Sarkar & Vafa (2024), "Lookahead bias in pretrained language models"; Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu (2025), "The memorization problem" | A model trained after the test period can recall the outcome. Masking names and dates does not stop it, and prompting it to "ignore the future" does not either. There is no recall after the knowledge cutoff. | A (as a warning) |
| He, Lv, Manela & Wu (2025), "Chronologically consistent large language models" | ChronoBERT / ChronoGPT: one model per year-end vintage, each trained only on text dated before its cutoff. Close to BERT on GLUE and comparable to Llama-3.1-8B in their news-return test. Free on Hugging Face (`manelalab/chrono-bert-v1-YYYY1231`). | B |

**Use here.** A "Lazy Prices" change score, read by an LLM: the Item 1A
risk-factor section of each 10-K, embedded with
`manelalab/chrono-bert-v1-20161231`, compared with the same firm's
previous 10-K. Names whose text changed most (bottom similarity quintile)
are excluded from the B50 sleeve, which is the direction the paper found.
The horizon (months) matches the book. Headline sentiment is not re-tried.

## 3. LLM-generated factor ideas tested by the algorithm (Alpha-GPT style)

| reference | finding | grade |
|---|---|---|
| Wang et al. (2023), "Alpha-GPT: human-AI interactive alpha mining" | An LLM turns ideas into formulaic alphas that a backtester then scores. Results are in-sample on Chinese A-shares. | C |
| Tang et al. (2025, KDD), "AlphaAgent: LLM-driven alpha mining with regularized exploration to counteract alpha decay" | Penalising factors that resemble known ones and capping formula complexity slows decay. Reports alpha on CSI 500 and S&P 500 over four recent years. The LLMs used were trained on those years. | C |
| Li et al. (2026, KDD), "Can LLM-based financial investing strategies outperform the market in long run?" (FINSABER) | Over two decades and 100+ stocks, the reported advantages of LLM trading agents mostly vanish once survivorship and data snooping are handled. | B (negative) |
| Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016, RFS), "...and the cross-section of expected returns" | With hundreds of factors tried, a new factor needs t > 3. | A |

**Use here.** The LLM writes hypotheses only. A local, open-weights
instruct model proposes formulas in a small whitelisted language over
point-in-time fields (prices, volume, the vq and tilt fundamentals). It
never sees returns or tickers and never places trades. The algorithm
parses each formula and rejects anything that reads the future. It then
screens each factor every January on the trailing five years: rank-IC
t > 2 on non-overlapping months, and correlation with the B50 composite
below 0.7. Factors that pass enter the sleeve ranking for that year
only. Every proposal screened in every year counts as a DSR trial.

## 4. Regime detection

| reference | finding | grade |
|---|---|---|
| Hamilton (1989); Ang & Bekaert (2002); Kritzman, Page & Turkington (2012, FAJ), "Regime shifts" | Markov-switching models describe turbulent vs calm states well in-sample. | B |
| Nystrup et al. (2018, JPM), "Dynamic allocation or diversification: a regime-based approach to multiple assets" | Out-of-sample HMM allocation improves drawdowns modestly; gains are sensitive to estimation windows. | B |
| Daniel & Moskowitz (2016, JFE), "Momentum crashes" | Momentum crashes are partly forecastable: they cluster in rebounds after bear markets with high volatility. | A |
| Moreira & Muir (2017, JF); Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (2020, JFE) | Vol-managed portfolios look good in-sample; real-time versions rarely beat the unmanaged ones. | B |

**Use here.** Regimes do not de-risk the book (phase 10's crash overlay
whipsawed). The phase-10 stress flag (SPY below its 200-day average AND
21-day vol above its trailing 80th percentile) is used only inside the
learning layer. In stress, the meta-weights reset to their prior instead
of chasing recent winners. That is the Daniel-Moskowitz point: recent
winners are least reliable right after a crash.

## 5. Online / adaptive model weighting

| reference | finding | grade |
|---|---|---|
| Timmermann (2006), "Forecast combinations" (Handbook of Economic Forecasting); Smith & Wallis (2009) | Estimated combination weights rarely beat equal weights out of sample (the "forecast combination puzzle"), because the estimation noise costs more than the weights gain. | A |
| DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009, RFS), "Optimal versus naive diversification" | 1/N beats most optimised allocations out of sample. | A |
| Ehsani & Linnainmaa (2022, JF), "Factor momentum and the momentum factor"; Gupta & Kelly (2019) | Factors with good past-year returns tend to keep earning; the effect is strongest at the 1-12 month horizon. | A/B |
| Helmbold, Schapire, Singer & Warmuth (1998), exponentiated-gradient portfolios | Multiplicative (Hedge-style) weight updates have regret bounds, i.e. they track the best fixed mix without estimating a covariance. | A (theory) |

**Use here.** Phase 9's monthly IC-weighted factor stack lost to fixed
50/50 weights (−6.7 pt/yr), which is the combination puzzle again. Phase
11 adapts at the level of whole sleeves instead of factor legs. It uses a
Hedge update on cost-free shadow-sleeve returns, half-shrunk to equal
weights, with a drift monitor that falls back to honest B50. Expect it,
at best, to avoid the worst sleeve. It is unlikely to beat the best
fixed sleeve.

## 6. How the LLM look-ahead is handled (stated as required)

Two different LLM roles, two different rules:

1. **Text scoring uses a model whose training cutoff precedes every
   score.** The 10-K embeddings use `chrono-bert-v1-20161231`, trained
   only on text dated before 2017-01-01. Every phase-11 signal date is
   2017-01-03 or later, so the model cannot know any outcome it is used
   to predict. One fixed vintage is used for all years, which keeps the
   embedding space comparable. The cost is a model that has not seen
   post-2016 vocabulary. That cost is accuracy, not bias.
2. **Factor proposals come from a model trained after most dev years.**
   The proposer is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (open weights, run locally on
   the Actions CPU). It was trained before its September 2024 release, so
   it has seen 2017-2023 and may "know" which kinds of factor worked.
   Its dev-window results are therefore **contaminated** and are not used
   as evidence. Only the sealed holdout (2025-11-24 to 2026-11-23,
   after the model's release) is a clean test of LLM-proposed factors.
   The proposals are committed before the dev run and never revised
   after seeing results.

A third, unavoidable leak is the researcher. This design was written by
an LLM assistant that knows the 2017-2024 factor literature, after phase
10 published the holdout returns of B50, SPY and the factor ETFs. That
is why the holdout is called re-viewed for those books, and why the
adoption rule also requires DSR ≥ 0.95 on the full sample.

## 7. Shortlist carried into phase 11

| idea | grade | new vs phases 9-10 | expected effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| EBIT/TEV + gross profitability + ROA tilt (QV) | A/B | new fundamentals (EBIT, assets, debt, cash, GP) | small premium, long droughts |
| Lazy-prices risk-factor change filter via ChronoBERT-2016 | A/B | first filing-text signal in the repo; leak-free model | removes some losers; small |
| LLM-proposed formulaic factors, algorithmic screen | C | new | likely noise; holdout is the only clean test |
| Stress-aware Hedge weights over sleeves + drift fallback | B | sleeve-level, not leg-level (phase 9) | avoids worst sleeve at best |

Rejected without a run: LLM trading agents (FINSABER is negative and
every candidate model has seen the test years), headline sentiment
(phase 9, wrong horizon), Markov-switching de-risking (phase-10 overlay
whipsaw, Cederburg et al.).
